# Distribution and Advisor Playbook

> Synthetic reference document for the AgentForge demo. Not a real sales playbook.

## Client segments and service tiers

The distribution team serves retail, high-net-worth, and institutional clients. **Institutional** clients receive a dedicated relationship team, monthly portfolio reviews, custom reporting, and an assigned service manager. Priority clients receive quarterly reviews and a four-hour response target. Essential clients use digital self-service and annual reviews.

## Advisor support standards

Advisors must respond to client **email within one business day** and return telephone calls the same business day. Client-facing materials must use approved content and current performance data. An advisor must not make a suitability recommendation without understanding the client's objectives, time horizon, and risk tolerance.

## Campaign measurement

Campaign performance is measured with approved attribution rules. A campaign report must state the measurement window, audience, channel, and definition of a qualified opportunity. The team must not imply guaranteed returns or use hypothetical performance without the required disclosures. Client contact preferences and consent are respected.

## Client context

The CRM is the authoritative source for segment, service tier, advisor, and relationship history. Client data is classified confidential and access is role-based. Agents may summarize approved CRM fields but must not infer health conditions, ethnicity, religion, or other sensitive traits. Any client-specific recommendation requires advisor review.

## Referrals and complaints

A referral is recorded with source, consent, and date. A complaint follows the Client Service Standards: acknowledgement within two business days and a target resolution within ten business days. A complaint about advice suitability is escalated to Compliance immediately.

## Metrics

Distribution leaders review client retention, net new assets, meeting coverage, campaign contribution, and service quality. Metrics are sourced from certified data products. A metric definition cannot be changed without approval from the Data Governance Council.
